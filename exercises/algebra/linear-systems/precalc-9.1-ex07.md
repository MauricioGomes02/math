# Is (−6, 1) a solution?

## Problem

Determine whether $(-6, 1)$ is a solution of the system

$$
\begin{cases}
-3x - 5y = 13 \\
-x + 4y = 10
\end{cases}
$$

## My attempt

**First equation** ($-3x - 5y = 13$):

$$
-3(-6) - 5(1) = 18 - 5 = 13 \ \checkmark
$$

**Second equation** ($-x + 4y = 10$):

$$
-(-6) + 4(1) = 6 + 4 = 10 \ \checkmark
$$

**Answer.** $(-6,\ 1)$ satisfies both equations, so it **is** a solution of the system.

## Mistake / lesson

- **Substitute from the problem statement, not from the previous line.** In the first attempt I
  copied the first equation's calculation and kept $-5y$ instead of the second equation's $4y$.
- **Use parentheses when substituting a negative number.** With $x = -6$, the term $-x$ becomes
  $-(-6) = 6$, not $-6$.
